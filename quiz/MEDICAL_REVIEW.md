# First Aid Quiz — medical author review

**Publication status: medical sign-off required.** The quiz follows the supplied wording and answer key. Several keyed answers and explanations conflict with first-aid guidance. The medical author should approve revisions before the quiz is published or used to teach first aid.

Question numbers below follow the interface. The source skips question 12, so quiz questions 12–14 correspond to source questions 13–15.

## Critical items

| Quiz question | Concern | Suggested correction for approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **02 · Breathing check** | The key marks all three choices. Phone-screen condensation and an Apple Watch cannot establish normal breathing and could delay recognition of cardiac arrest. The explanation recommends phone fogging in the very situation where weak or abnormal breathing needs urgent assessment. | Key **B** only. Open the airway and look, listen and feel for normal breathing for up to 10 seconds. Call emergency services promptly for an unresponsive adult. If breathing is absent or abnormal, including gasping, start CPR and use an AED as soon as available. [St John Ambulance](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/cpr/), [Resuscitation Council UK](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/adult-basic-life-support-guidelines), [Apple Blood Oxygen app limitations](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/120358) |
| **03 · Suspected overdose** | Choice A and the explanation encourage inducing vomiting after a likely overdose. An unconscious person can choke or aspirate. | Key **B** only. Call emergency services. If they are breathing normally, use the recovery position and keep checking breathing. Avoid inducing vomiting or giving anything by mouth. Keep the packaging for responders if available. [NHS poisoning guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/poisoning/) |
| **04 · Live wire** | The keyed action relies on a wooden chair being safe despite unknown voltage, moisture and scene conditions. It bypasses the nearby breaker. A sustained electrically induced muscle contraction and a seizure are distinct clinical concepts. | Replace the keyed action with switching off the electrical supply at the breaker if safe, then following the dispatcher’s instructions. Keep clear of the casualty and source until power is off. Stay away from high-voltage or downed lines and wait for responders. Describe involuntary muscle contraction without calling it a seizure. [St John Ambulance](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/electrocution/), [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/first-aid/first-aid-electrical-shock/basics/art-20056695) |
| **05 · Drowning** | The explanation encourages an untrained bystander to enter the water if a ring is unavailable. Summer conditions do not make that safe. The “100 %” claim has no basis. | Retain the shoreline throw as the keyed action. Call for help, stay out of the water, and reach or throw from a safe position. Advising a responsive person to float can be an additional measure while help is arranged. [RNLI water safety guidance](https://rnli.org/news-and-media/2026/june/11/rnli-urges-families-to-share-safety-advice-to-prevent-more-drowning-tragedies) |
| **07 · Head injury** | Difficulty staying awake is an emergency sign. The explanation’s cross-reference leaves assessment and escalation implicit. “Leave them alone” can be read as walking away. | Call emergency services. Stay with them and minimise movement because a neck injury may be present. Monitor breathing, follow the dispatcher’s instructions, and start CPR if they become unresponsive and are not breathing normally. [NHS head injury guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/), [Resuscitation Council UK first aid guidelines](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/first-aid-guidelines) |
| **08 · Chemical splash** | The explanation endorses vinegar on skin, which can delay irrigation or worsen a chemical injury. | Keep **B**. Start rinsing the eye immediately with plenty of clean water for at least 20 minutes and get urgent medical help. Continue rinsing as directed. Avoid using vinegar or other chemicals to neutralize a splash on the skin or in the eye. [NHS eye injuries](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/eye-injuries/), [NHS chemical burns](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acid-and-chemical-burns/) |
| **11 · Unconscious person with insulin pen** | The explanation instructs giving oral sugar to an unconscious person. This risks choking or aspiration regardless of their actual glucose level. An insulin pen does not establish the cause of unconsciousness. | Keep **C** for someone breathing normally. Call emergency services, monitor breathing and give nothing by mouth. Glucagon may be appropriate for suspected severe hypoglycaemia if available and the responder knows how to use it. [NHS low blood sugar guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/low-blood-sugar-hypoglycaemia/) |
| **13 · Venomous snakebite** | The only keyed response is taking a photo, which could lead to another bite or delay emergency care. A photo may help with identification, but clinicians determine whether antivenom is needed. | Replace the keyed action with getting clear of the snake, calling emergency services immediately and keeping still. A photo is optional from an already safe distance if it takes no time away from care. Avoid approaching the snake or trying to suck out venom. [CDC / NIOSH snakebite guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/outdoor-workers/about/venomous-snakes.html) |
| **14 · Unresponsive after a party** | The explanation stops after the breathing check. Snoring-like, slow or irregular gasps can be mistaken for normal breathing, delaying CPR. Alcohol should not be assumed to explain loss of consciousness. | Retain an explicit check for **normal** breathing. Call emergency services immediately for an unresponsive adult. Start CPR and use an AED if breathing is absent or abnormal. If they breathe normally and no trauma is suspected, use the recovery position and continue monitoring. [Resuscitation Council UK adult basic life support](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/adult-basic-life-support-guidelines), [Resuscitation Council UK first aid](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/first-aid-guidelines) |

## Other items to clarify

| Quiz location | Concern | Suggested correction for approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Introduction · 112** | The fictional assumption that help is coming is part of the classroom setup. International readers may have a different emergency number. | Keep the fictional setup and state separately that in a real emergency the reader should dial the local emergency number. 112 is available throughout the EU. [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112) |
| **01 · Road traffic injury** | “Must not move” is too absolute. The scene could become unsafe or the casualty may need airway care or CPR. | Confirm that the scene is safe, avoid unnecessary movement, keep the person warm and monitor them. Follow emergency instructions if there is immediate danger or lifesaving care requires movement. [Resuscitation Council UK first aid](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/first-aid-guidelines) |
| **06 · Possible stroke** | The symptoms warrant urgent action and are insufficient for a definitive diagnosis. Onset may be unknown. | Describe a suspected stroke. Call emergency services immediately, record symptom onset or the last time the person was known to be well, and retain the B.E. F.A.S.T. mnemonic. [American Stroke Association](https://www.stroke.org/en/be-fast-experience/time-to-call-911), [CDC stroke signs](https://www.cdc.gov/stroke/signs-symptoms/), [American Stroke Association EMS form](https://www.stroke.org/-/media/Stroke-Files/EMS-Resources/EMS-Stroke-Alert-Form.pdf) |
| **09 · Knocked-out tooth** | The question asks about putting a tooth back, but the key covers transport only. Replanting a baby tooth can harm the developing permanent tooth. | Specify a permanent tooth. Handle by the crown, gently reinsert it when safe and feasible, or store it in milk or sterile saline and get emergency dental care immediately. Never reinsert a baby tooth. [NHS knocked-out tooth](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/knocked-out-tooth/), [International Association of Dental Traumatology](https://www.aapd.org/media/Policies_Guidelines/E_Avulsion.pdf) |
| **10 · Chest pressure** | The scenario cannot establish a specific type of myocardial infarction. Waiting to see if it passes risks delaying care. | Describe a possible heart attack, call emergency services immediately, keep the person comfortable at rest and stay with them. [NHS heart attack guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/) |
| **12 · Attached tick** | The explanation contradicts the upward-pull answer by advising twisting. It overstates the harm of retained mouthparts. Tick-borne encephalitis vaccination is preventive and does not address the current bite. | Keep **B**. Grip close to the skin with fine-tipped tweezers and pull straight up steadily. If small mouthparts remain and cannot be removed easily, leave them alone. Clean the site and seek medical advice if symptoms develop. Discuss vaccination separately for future exposures based on location and risk. [CDC tick removal](https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/after-a-tick-bite/index.html), [CDC tick-borne encephalitis prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/tick-borne-encephalitis/prevention/) |
